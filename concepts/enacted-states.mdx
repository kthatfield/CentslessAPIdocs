## What reading the enacted text changed

The engine's dataset was first built from the Centsless Legislative Observatory's October 18 record and then checked against every enacted text. The differences:

- **Three states listed as "method per statute" use the standard symmetrical table** (Connecticut, Minnesota, Oregon). Only New Mexico leaves the method to an agency rule.
- **Oklahoma's state-entity mandate is at 74 O.S. 3120**, not 62 O.S. 9002, and excludes tax levies. Section 9002 covers political subdivisions: permissive from November 1, 2026 and mandatory from July 1, 2027. The congressional-deference clause attaches to 9002 only.
- **Small totals differ by state.** Georgia, Hawaii, Minnesota and Nebraska round $0.01 and $0.02 totals up to $0.05; Maryland rounds anything under $0.05 up to $0.05; Connecticut exempts totals of four cents or less; California exempts totals of two cents or less. The symmetrical table alone would round a one- or two-cent total down to zero. The engine applies each state's rule.
- **Vermont requires cash refunds in the exact amount paid.** The engine does not round Vermont refunds.
- **Scope beyond retail.** Hawaii, Maryland and Nebraska reach cash wages; Vermont excludes them. Alabama excludes payments to governmental entities. Oregon and Washington have separate public-body provisions.
- **Posted notices** are required in Arizona (prescribed wording), Oregon (signage), Vermont (model notice) and Minnesota (agency policy). The engine records `notice_required` on the rule.
- **Activation conditions** appear in Florida, Tennessee and North Carolina (the penny no longer in production), Arizona (coins not available or not used), Idaho and Tennessee (exact change unavailable) and Washington's agency section. The engine records each condition and its basis in the sealed record.
- **North Carolina** has three rules, **Kentucky** four sections, **Indiana** two chapters with mandatory rounding and an operator's choice of method, and **Virginia**'s government provision is a temporary local-ordinance authority.

## Not in the dataset as enacted

The table also carries the Observatory's non-binding records so that a status change is one row update: New York (passed both chambers, awaiting delivery to the Governor), Pennsylvania (passed one chamber), Massachusetts, Michigan, New Jersey and Ohio (introduced), South Carolina, Texas and Wisconsin (guidance), Wyoming (drafting), and Alaska, Iowa, Mississippi and West Virginia (failed). None of these rows ever rounds.

## Items still pending a clean citation

Four details could not be confirmed from a final codified text on October 25 and are marked in the record notes: Nebraska's and Hawaii's statute numbers (assigned by their revisors after the slip law), Tennessee's new Title 47 chapter 50 section number, and the clean wording of Maryland Bus.
